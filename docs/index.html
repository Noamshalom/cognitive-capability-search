What can this learner become?

Evolution never designed the brain to read, yet culture found an input the brain learns so well that it becomes automatic. Cognitive Capability Search asks the next question as a search problem: keep a learner fixed, and search over interfaces and training regimes for new abilities it can acquire, without ever naming the ability in advance.

Why search for abilities instead of teaching them?

Reading is a few thousand years old, far too recent to have evolved. It works because writing happens to fit circuitry that already existed. The brain recycles it. Sensory substitution makes the same point from the other side: a vibrating belt can become a sense of north, and rats can learn to perceive infrared light.

In each of those cases someone guessed the ability first and then built the interface. CCS reverses the order.

Not “how do I teach it X?” but “which X are within its reach, and at what cost to what it already does?”

How the search works

An inner loop is ordinary learning. An outer loop searches over the environment: which new input channel the learner gets, how that channel encodes the world, what self-supervised signal it trains on, whether its old sense is on while it learns, and which of its regions are plastic and for how long. Each candidate is scored by what the learner can newly read out of the world, minus what it lost.

score(e) = capability gain G(e) − β · interference I(e) − γ · plasticity time T(e)

The learner “develops” on its innate sense, which sees only latents z0–z3 of an 8-latent world. Its interface region Rc starts with a pathway into the association region R2 that sits below R2’s noise floor (dashed). A new ability means some of z4–z7 becomes readable from R2.

The finding we didn’t design in

Learn the new channel while the old sense is silent.

All twelve winners chose to train with the innate sense switched off. The hand-designed “obvious” environment did the opposite and wrecked the learner’s existing ability. Take the winners, change only this one setting, and watch what happens.

Input during learning

Means over 3 worlds × 3 fresh seeds. Mechanism, measured: with the sense off, the sensory weights receive exactly zero gradient and the association region’s input-dependent change is 0.10 SD instead of 0.79. A revert test shows the damage in the ablated condition lives in the sensory weights (restoring W1 alone recovers 0.83 of innate R²). The search rediscovered gradient isolation, the idea behind weight freezing and progressive networks, by choosing an input regime, without knowing the architecture. In hindsight the principle is familiar; what the search contributes is finding it, where the “obvious” hand-designed regime missed it.

What didn’t work
Hypothesis not supported

Smart plasticity didn’t beat global plasticity.

Region-level control (“open R2 briefly, then freeze”) scored 0.499 against 0.513 for opening everything. Input gating did the protective work instead. Plasticity control only mattered as a second line of defence: with gating removed, it kept damage at 0.35 versus 0.83.

Dead end

The new region alone can’t do it.

Opening only the interface region plateaued at a score of about 0.19. It cannot amplify a pathway into the association region that starts below the noise floor. A new ability needed the association region itself to change.

Metric gamed

The search optimised exactly what I measured.

Interference was measured with the new channel off. With it on, winners’ innate R² drops from 0.97 to 0.88. The score never penalised that crosstalk, so the search didn’t avoid it. Closing the gap (below) didn’t make the search find a crosstalk-free solution either: it simply paid the penalty.

Replication · 10 worlds, corrected metric

Everything survives the gap being closed.

The study was re-run in ten independently generated worlds with interference measured as the worse of channel-off and channel-on. CCS beats random search in 10 of 10 worlds (adaptive, sign test p = 0.002) and 9 of 10 (global). All 20 winners again chose channel-only input and a linear code. Adaptive and global plasticity remain indistinguishable (adaptive better in 4 of 10). What changed: the winners now carry a crosstalk penalty (interference ≈ 0.07–0.08) for essentially the same gain. Within this environment space, some crosstalk seems to be the price of a new channel entering a shared region at all.

What this shows, and what it doesn’t
What it shows

For a fixed learner, the space of interfaces and training regimes can be searched for capabilities rather than for a task. The search finds regions far better than random sampling, what it finds transfers to tasks it never saw, and it surfaces training principles a designer can then test directly.

What it doesn’t

Anything about human brains. The learner is a three-region tanh network, “capability” means linear decodability of a synthetic world’s factors, the environment space is one we designed, and 240 evaluations per search is small.

Why it might matter

Near term, CCS could search the design space of sensory-substitution devices, BCIs and curricula with an explicit interference budget. The far question: are there modalities with no cultural analogue yet, like perceiving another person’s neural state as ordinary input? If so, they would be sitting undiscovered because nobody searched.
